About

Innovation as a way to give back.

I'm an Industrial Engineering student and Egleston Scholar at Columbia University, with a minor in Artificial Intelligence. My work encompasses engineering, sustainability, marketing strategy, and venture growth.

My portfolio includes molecular modeling of modified curcumin derivatives using Schrödinger Maestro to explore cancer-pathway inhibition, and a Passive Earth Air Tunnel System built with Arduino sensors and statistical analysis to cool classrooms in Puerto Rico. Beyond the lab, I've merged data-based decision-making with a creative lens helping grow Puerto Rico's fastest-rising wellness influencer through brand positioning, content strategy, and partnerships.

As the first RISE Fellow from Puerto Rico, I shared my story at the RISE Summit in Oxford speaking about how growing up on the island shaped my passion for sustainable engineering and solutions rooted in uplifting under-resourced communities.

“Using innovation as a way to give back to the communities that have shaped me.”

Molecular Modeling of Modified Curcumin

Designed and docked curcumin derivatives targeting the PI3K p110 subunit using Schrödinger Maestro — assessing binding affinity to explore potential cancer-pathway inhibition. A computational chemistry project bridging molecular design and data-driven evaluation.

Schrödinger Maestro Molecular Docking Drug Discovery Computational Chemistry
Robinson School · Innovation Lab

Passive Earth Air Tunnel System

Designed and tested a passive cooling system to reduce classroom temperatures in Puerto Rico, using Arduino sensors and statistical analysis to confirm measurable temperature reduction — sustainable climate adaptation with no energy cost.

Arduino Sensors Sustainability Statistical Analysis

NYC Air Pollution Analyzer

Built a Python ETL pipeline to parse and process air-quality data from CSV files, paired with a CLI tool for date- and location-based queries across all 42 NYC UHF districts.
